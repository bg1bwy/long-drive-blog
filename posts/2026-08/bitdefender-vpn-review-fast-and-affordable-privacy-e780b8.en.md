Wired recently reviewed Bitdefender VPN, highlighting its affordable pricing and solid baseline privacy, though it notes a lack of advanced features for hardcore enthusiasts. This focus on digital privacy reminds us that protecting data in transit is only half the battle.

While privacy tools help keep your information secure during transmission, truly safeguarding your digital legacy requires a different approach: reliable, long-term storage. Whether you are archiving corporate records, preserving family photos, or backing up critical documents, the longevity of your data is just as important as its confidentiality. Traditional cloud services often rely on recurring subscriptions that can expire, leaving files vulnerable to deletion or loss over time. As our lives become increasingly digital, the need for a storage solution that matches the permanence of our memories and records has never been more apparent. Combining privacy with durability ensures your assets remain both secure and accessible for years to come.

This is where permanent storage architectures like Long Drive provide a practical answer. Built on the Arweave blockchain and operated by Long Hash Inc., Japan, Long Drive enables users to upload files once and store them indefinitely through a prepaid fee model. Your data is distributed across a decentralized network, ensuring it never disappears due to server shutdowns or policy changes. The prepaid model eliminates the risk of service suspension, while the cryptographic proof of storage guarantees that your files remain intact without relying on centralized maintenance. For sensitive information, the platform offers optional client-side AES-256-GCM encryption, meaning your private key is password-protected and completely inaccessible even to the service operators. New accounts receive 10 MB of lifetime free storage, while any file under 100 KB remains free forever. With interfaces available in Japanese, English, and Chinese, it caters to a diverse audience seeking durability for corporate archives, important documents, and long-term media preservation.
