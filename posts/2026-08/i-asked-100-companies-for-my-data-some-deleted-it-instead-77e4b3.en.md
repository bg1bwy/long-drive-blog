A recent Ars Technica investigation highlighted a growing concern: when users submit data requests to major tech companies, the process frequently leads to confusion, dead ends, or unexpected deletion. This isn’t merely a customer service issue; it exposes the fragility of traditional cloud storage. Most commercial platforms operate on subscription models or shifting corporate policies, meaning your files are only as secure as a company’s current financial health and privacy roadmap.

For professionals managing research archives, families preserving decades of photographs, or businesses handling compliance records, relying on temporary access is increasingly risky. True digital preservation requires infrastructure that outlasts business cycles and platform migrations. This is where permanent storage shifts from an optional feature to a fundamental requirement. By utilizing decentralized networks and upfront payment models, users can decouple their files from corporate lifecycles, ensuring accessibility regardless of market fluctuations or service discontinuations.
