Recent research published on Ars Technica reveals that medieval manuscripts are serving as unexpected "biological time capsules," preserving genetic traces of the deadly sheeppox virus. As experts note, archives and libraries worldwide may hold similar historical data from past disease outbreaks.

This discovery highlights a profound truth: preserving physical history requires durable media, yet our modern digital records face an equally urgent need for long-term preservation. Traditional cloud storage often relies on recurring subscriptions or centralized servers that can change policies, shut down, or suffer from bit rot over decades. When critical corporate records, personal archives, or scientific data are left in temporary digital environments, they risk disappearing just as quickly as the next trend fades. If we want future researchers to access today’s information accurately, we need infrastructure designed to outlast business cycles and technological shifts.
